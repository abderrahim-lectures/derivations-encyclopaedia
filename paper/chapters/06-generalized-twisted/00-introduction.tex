% STATUS: written 2026-11-04 (Bresar 1991 read in full) A derivation follows
the product rule. Several lines of work weaken that rule, and each weakening
starts from a different question \cite[p.~89]{Bresar1991Glasgow}. The first
line began with a question about norms \cite[p.~89]{Bresar1991Glasgow}. Brešar asked how far the composition
of two derivations sits from a wider class of maps, and he called that class
the generalized derivations \cite[p.~89]{Bresar1991Glasgow}. A second line
keeps the rule but turns it: multiplication on one side is twisted by an
endomorphism, and the twisted derivations arise
(Section~\ref{sec:generalized-twisted:ore-extensions}). Each of these classes then
developed a life of its own, with its own Posner-type questions, its own
commutativity theorems, and its own operator examples.
% claim-ok: the plan of this chapter

This chapter follows the lines in turn.

Section
\ref{sec:generalized-twisted:generalized} reports the generalized derivations,
from the norm estimate where they were named to the algebraic study of their
products and commutator identities. The sections after it take up the twists.
